Learn Piano in Your 60s: It Is Not Too Late, and Here Is the Plan

Retirement gives you the one thing a pianist needs most: unhurried time. This course gives you the order in which to use it.

People begin in their sixties because there is finally time, or because a parent's piano is now in their living room, or because they have wanted to play for fifty years. All three are good reasons.

The lessons are free, in order, and unhurried. You can pause, repeat and come back next week without losing anything, and when you are ready you can send a recording and hear from me in writing.

Be honest about the obstacles

What is actually in your way in your sixties

Fingers that are slower than you want

Speed is the last thing to arrive and the least important. The first goals are an even sound and a relaxed hand, and both are achievable at any age. If a joint hurts, check with your doctor first.

Reading music with tired eyes

Choose editions with large print, put a lamp on the music and stand the book higher. Reading is learned in small steps, and the course introduces each symbol only when you need it.

Remembering what you learned yesterday

Short daily practice beats long weekly sessions. A one-line log of what you practiced keeps the thread.

Worry that it is too late

It is not. Adult beginners in their sixties and seventies play real music with real expression. The goal is not a concert career; it is playing the music you love.

A keyboard that is wrong for you

Weighted keys and a sturdy bench make a bigger difference than people expect. My gear page explains what to choose and what to avoid.

A realistic start

Your first three months

Week 1

Lesson 1, no pressure. Sit comfortably, find the notes and play your first melody by ear.

Months 1 to 3

One lesson at a time. Twenty minutes a day is enough. Rewatch freely.

Month 3

Send a recording for feedback. Choose a piece from the list that you have always wanted to play.

Questions adults ask in their 60s

Is it too late to start piano at 65?

No. Many adults start in their sixties and play real pieces within months. The course moves at your pace and nothing in it is timed.

What is the easiest way to start learning piano in retirement?

Follow a structured course in order, use a weighted keyboard, practice a little every day and get feedback on a recording. That sequence works better than random videos.

Can I play Let It Be or Moon River as a beginner?

Yes, after the first stages of the course. Both rely on chords and a calm left hand, and both are very common first goals.
