Roland FP-10 vs Yamaha P-145BT: A Piano Teacher's Comparison

For an adult beginner, I would choose the Roland FP-10 if key feel is your priority, and the Yamaha P-145BT if you want a lighter body and Bluetooth audio. Both have 88 keys and a pedal switch in the box. The FP-10 has 96-voice polyphony and the escapement action; the P-145BT has 64-note polyphony and weighs 11.1 kg to the FP-10's 12.6 kg.

The keys

Both are graded: heavier in the bass, lighter in the treble. The FP-10 adds escapement, a small notch you feel as the key lets go, and ivory-feel white keys. These help with quiet playing and repeated notes. The Yamaha's GHC keyboard is simpler, and a beginner will not miss what they have never felt.

Polyphony and sound

Polyphony counts how many notes can sound at once. At 96 the FP-10 has more room than the P-145BT's 64. For first-year pieces neither will run out; for long pedaled passages, the FP-10 has more headroom. For more, see Yamaha P-145 vs P-225.

Connections

The P-145BT offers Bluetooth audio, so you can play along with a recording through the piano's speakers. The FP-10 has Bluetooth MIDI, which works with apps, though Roland notes it may not be included depending on the country of purchase.

Roland FP-10

Who it is for: An adult beginner in a small room who wants a proper piano action and nothing extra.

What the listing says: Roland lists 88 keys on its PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and ivory-feel white keys, 96-voice polyphony, two 12 cm speakers, one headphone jack, a pedal switch in the box and a weight of 12.6 kg without the music rest.

What I look for: Whether the keys feel graded (heavier in the bass, lighter in the treble), whether quiet notes sound at the touch you meant, and whether a repeated note answers quickly. Escapement, the small notch you feel as the key lets go of the hammer, is the first thing I check on a beginner piano because it teaches control of soft playing.

My choice if you will be on headphones and the touch is the priority.

Pros
  • Real graded action in a compact, light body
  • Simple controls that do not distract a beginner
Cons
  • 96-voice polyphony runs out sooner with long pedaled passages
  • One headphone jack, so a teacher cannot listen alongside you

Buy it if you want the best key feel for a modest budget and a small footprint. Skip it if you already know you will play pedal-heavy Romantic pieces, or you want a second headphone jack.

Yamaha P-145BT

Who it is for: An adult beginner who plays along with apps and wants the right keys without paying for extras.

What the listing says: Yamaha lists 88 keys on a GHC keyboard with matte black keytops, four touch settings (hard, medium, soft, fixed), 64-note polyphony, 10 voices, Bluetooth audio, one headphone jack, a footswitch in the box and a weight of 11.1 kg.

What I look for: A graded keyboard with a surface that does not slip, and a touch setting I can leave on medium and forget. For a first piano I would rather see a good action and few voices than the reverse.

My choice if you also play along with recordings and carry the piano.

Pros
  • Graded-hammer-style keyboard in a light body
  • Bluetooth audio for playing along with a recording
Cons
  • 64-note polyphony is the lowest in this class
  • Only 10 voices

Buy it if you want one right instrument for the first few years and plan to spend the savings on a pedal and a bench. Skip it if you want two headphone jacks or a bigger sound for pedal-heavy repertoire.

Which should you buy?

If you can only try one in a shop, try the FP-10 first, then choose by feel. If you cannot try either, the P-145BT is the safer first piano because it is lighter and plays audio. Whichever you choose, add an adjustable bench and a proper pedal.

Questions adults ask

Which is better for a beginner, the FP-10 or the P-145BT?

Both have 88 graded keys and are good first pianos. The FP-10 has the more refined key action; the P-145BT is lighter and has Bluetooth audio.

Do either come with a pedal?

Both come with a pedal switch. A proper piano-style pedal is a small upgrade.

Do I need Bluetooth?

Not to learn. It is convenient for playing along with a recording.
