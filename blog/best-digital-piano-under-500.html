Quick picks

  • Best overall: Roland FP-10 — the PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement
  • Best value: Yamaha P-45 — weighted keys from a major maker at a low entry point
  • Tightest budget: Donner DEP-20 — 88 full-size weighted keys, though with less detail on the listing

How I choose

At this level the key action decides everything. I look for the words weighted, graded or hammer action in the listing, and I treat semi-weighted as a different instrument. I have not played every instrument or used every device in this guide, and I do not say that I have. What I give you is what I look for as a concert pianist who teaches adults, checked against what each Amazon listing and manufacturer page actually states.

The picks

Roland FP-10

Who it is for: An adult beginner in a small room who wants a proper piano action and nothing extra.

What the listing says: Roland lists 88 keys on its PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and ivory-feel white keys, 96-voice polyphony, two 12 cm speakers, one headphone jack, a pedal switch in the box and a weight of 12.6 kg without the music rest.

What I look for: Whether the keys feel graded (heavier in the bass, lighter in the treble), whether quiet notes sound at the touch you meant, and whether a repeated note answers quickly. Escapement, the small notch you feel as the key lets go of the hammer, is the first thing I check on a beginner piano because it teaches control of soft playing.

The best touch I can point to under this ceiling, in a compact body.

Pros
  • Real graded action in a compact, light body
  • Simple controls that do not distract a beginner
Cons
  • 96-voice polyphony runs out sooner with long pedaled passages
  • One headphone jack, so a teacher cannot listen alongside you

Buy it if you want the best key feel for a modest budget and a small footprint. Skip it if you already know you will play pedal-heavy Romantic pieces, or you want a second headphone jack.

Yamaha P-45

Who it is for: A first-year beginner who needs weighted keys at the lowest sensible entry point.

What the listing says: The listing says 88 weighted keys, with a music rest and a sustain pedal.

What I look for: Weighted keys are the point. I would play a few slow five-finger patterns and listen for even volume from note to note.

A sensible first piano if you want a Yamaha. Read the listing carefully, because it states few specifications.

Pros
  • Weighted keys at an entry level
  • Music rest and sustain pedal listed with it
Cons
  • Plainer sound engine than the models above it
  • The listing gives few specifications, so check the page before you buy

Buy it if you want weighted keys and your budget is tight. Skip it if you want a richer sound or a quick path to Bluetooth and USB audio.

Donner DEP-20

Who it is for: A beginner on a strict budget who still wants weighted keys.

What the listing says: The listing says 88 full-size weighted keys at a budget price.

What I look for: I would play slowly and listen for uneven key response, because that is where budget actions show their limits.

Worth considering only when the budget is firm. I would choose a major maker if I could stretch.

Pros
  • Weighted full-size keys at a low entry point
  • Simple to set up
Cons
  • Less polished action and sound than the major makers
  • Little specification detail on the listing

Buy it if weighted keys are the one thing you cannot compromise on and budget is tight. Skip it if you can stretch to a Yamaha or Roland, whose key actions I know well.

What about semi-weighted keyboards?

Several 88-key keyboards at this level, such as the Alesis Recital and the Korg Liano, have semi-weighted keys. They are lighter and springier than a piano's hammer action. They will teach notes and rhythm; they teach less about control of soft and loud. Read weighted vs semi-weighted keys before you choose.

How to choose under $500

  • Choose weighted keys over a longer feature list.
  • Choose 88 keys over 61 if you can; see how many keys you need.
  • Save a little for a bench and a pedal.

If you can stretch, the Yamaha P-145BT and P-225 are the next step up.

Questions adults ask

Can I learn piano on a digital piano under $500?

Yes, if it has weighted keys. That is the feature that matters most for an adult building a real technique.

Is semi-weighted good enough?

For learning notes and rhythm, yes. For learning touch, a weighted or graded action is better.

What should I buy with it?

A sustain pedal, an adjustable bench and a pair of closed-back headphones.
