What should an adult beginner buy first?

The instrument, and within the instrument, the keys. Everything I teach in the first year, from arm weight to a firm fingertip to the difference between a note that sings and a note that is merely correct, depends on a key that pushes back the way a piano key does. I explain the difference in weighted versus semi-weighted keys. If you only have budget for one thing this season, buy a weighted 88-key digital piano and add the rest later.

If you are still deciding between a keyboard and a digital piano, read digital piano vs keyboard first. If you want my full ranking for beginners, it is in the best digital piano for adult beginners.

Which digital piano is worth watching this Black Friday?

These are the six I would put on a watch list. Specifications below are taken from the manufacturers' own pages (listed at the bottom).

Yamaha P-145BT

Who it is for: An adult starting from zero who wants one honest, compact instrument.

Yamaha lists 88 keys on its GHC keyboard, 64-note maximum polyphony, ten preset voices, Bluetooth audio, two 7 W amplifiers and a weight of 11.1 kg. It has one headphone jack and ships with a footswitch, so plan on buying a proper pedal. It is the one I would buy first, and I compare it directly with its bigger sibling in Yamaha P-145 vs P-225.

Check it on Amazon →

Yamaha P-225

Who it is for: The student who already knows they will stay with the piano and wants more sound and more headroom.

Yamaha lists the same 88-key GHC keyboard, but with 192-note maximum polyphony, 24 preset voices, two headphone jacks (handy when a teacher or friend wants to listen with you) and a weight of 11.5 kg. More polyphony matters most when you use the pedal heavily, because sustained notes keep counting against the limit.

Check it on Amazon →

Roland FP-30X

Who it is for: The student who values a fuller speaker sound and Roland's key feel.

Roland lists an 88-key PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and Ivory Feel, 256-note maximum polyphony, 11 W x 2 speakers, Bluetooth audio and two headphone jacks. At 14.8 kg with the music rest it is heavier than the Yamahas, which is fine if it lives in one place. The head-to-head is in Roland FP-30X vs Yamaha P-225.

Check it on Amazon →

Roland FP-10

Who it is for: The student with a small room and a small budget tier who still wants a real key action.

Roland lists the same PHA-4 Standard keyboard as the FP-30X, with 96-note maximum polyphony, 6 W x 2 speakers, one headphone jack and a pedal switch in the box. It is the leaner machine: the speakers are smaller and the polyphony lower, but the keys are the part you will practice on every day.

Check it on Amazon →

Yamaha P-45

Who it is for: A budget-conscious beginner who wants a proven weighted action.

Yamaha lists 88 keys with a graded hammer standard (GHS) keyboard, 64-note maximum polyphony, ten preset voices and a weight of 11.5 kg, and the package includes a pedal. It is an older, simpler design, and if the price is close to the P-145BT on the day, I would take the P-145BT.

Check it on Amazon →

Yamaha DGX-670

Who it is for: The student who wants accompaniment styles, hundreds of sounds and a bigger body.

Yamaha lists a GHS keyboard with 88 keys, 256-note maximum polyphony, 601 voices and a weight of 21.4 kg. I do not think a beginner needs 601 voices, but if the family wants one instrument that can also accompany singing, this is the one to watch.

Is a keyboard enough if my budget tier is small?

It can be a start, with honesty about its limits. A 61-key portable keyboard teaches note names, rhythm and reading, but it cannot teach you the touch of a piano. The Casio CT-S1 is the one I would look at: Casio lists 61 keys, three touch-sensitivity levels (or off), 64-note maximum polyphony and a weight of 4.5 kg without batteries. If you go this route, plan to move to a weighted instrument within the year. My full thinking is in the best keyboard for beginners, and more models are on the keyboards page.

Which accessories should I add on Black Friday?

Headphones, for daily practice without disturbing the house. Short sessions every day beat long sessions once a week, and headphones remove the excuses of late hours and thin walls. The Sennheiser HD 280 Pro is closed-back, and Sennheiser lists it at 285 g without the cable; the Audio-Technica ATH-M20x is closed-back and listed at 190 g without cable. Both come with a 6.3 mm adapter. See the best headphones for digital piano and the headphones page.

A real sustain pedal. The small footswitch that comes in many boxes works but does not feel like a pedal. The Yamaha FC4A is a piano-style foot switch, and the Roland DP-10 has a continuous/switch selector for half-pedal on Roland keyboards that support it. Read the sustain pedal guide before you choose, and see the pedals page.

A bench at the right height. This is the accessory people skip and then blame their wrists. The Yamaha PKBB1 adjustable bench lists a height range of 17.5 to 19.5 inches. My rules for sitting are in how to choose a piano bench, and more options are on the benches and stands page.

A way to record yourself. A handheld recorder or a USB microphone lets you send me your playing on the feedback page. The Zoom H1n has built-in 90-degree XY stereo microphones, and the Audio-Technica AT2020USB-X is a cardioid condenser USB microphone. See the best microphone for recording piano at home and the recording and video page.

How do I shop a sale without regret?

Use this order. First, decide the instrument tier in words: starter, mid-range or premium. Second, confirm weighted keys and 88 keys. Third, confirm the piano is sold and shipped by a seller you trust, and read the return terms. Fourth, add the pedal and bench in the same order, since they change how the piano feels. Fifth, ignore feature lists you will never use.

If the instrument you want is not discounted, do not buy the wrong one because it is. A weighted piano at today's price beats a lighter keyboard at a lower one, because the lighter keyboard is the one you will replace.

Questions adults ask

When is Black Friday 2026?

Black Friday is Friday, November 27, 2026, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Check today's price on Amazon before you buy, because prices change daily.

What is the best digital piano to buy on Black Friday as a beginner?

A weighted 88-key digital piano. My starting pick is the Yamaha P-145BT, with the Yamaha P-225 and Roland FP-30X as step-ups. Choose by key action first and price second.

Is a keyboard or a digital piano better for an adult beginner?

A weighted digital piano, because the key resistance is where touch and technique are learned. A 61-key keyboard is a workable start only if you plan to upgrade within the year.

What accessories do I need with a digital piano?

Headphones for daily practice, a proper sustain pedal, and an adjustable bench at the right height. A microphone or phone setup is useful for sending recordings of your playing for feedback.
