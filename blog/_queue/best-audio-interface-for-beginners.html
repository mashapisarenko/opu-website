Best Audio Interface for Beginners (2026): A Pianist's Guide

An audio interface is the box between your microphone and your computer. Here is how to choose a first one, and the ones I would look at.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 24, 2026

Forty-one lessons in order, free, at your own pace: Start at Lesson 1 →

Shopping for this? My picks, chosen by a concert pianist: Audio Interfaces and Microphones and Interfaces.

The best audio interface for a beginner is the Focusrite Scarlett Solo 4th Gen if you record one microphone, and the Scarlett 2i2 4th Gen if you want two. The Audient EVO 4 is a simple alternative, and the Arturia MiniFuse 2 adds MIDI and software.

Quick picks

  • Best overall: Focusrite Scarlett Solo 4th Gen — simple, bus-powered USB-C
  • Best for stereo recording: Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 4th Gen — two microphone preamps
  • Simple alternative: Audient EVO 4 — a compact 2-in 2-out with a clear layout

How I choose

A beginner needs an interface that is quiet and simple. I count the microphone preamps, because one decides whether you can record in stereo. I have not played every instrument or used every device in this guide, and I do not say that I have. What I give you is what I look for as a concert pianist who teaches adults, checked against what each Amazon listing and manufacturer page actually states.

The picks

Focusrite Scarlett Solo 4th Gen

Who it is for: A pianist who records one microphone, a stereo line from a digital piano, or a guitar-style instrument input, and wants a simple box.

What the listing says: Focusrite lists one microphone preamp, one instrument input and one line input, two balanced line outputs, one headphone output, 24-bit/192 kHz converters, phantom power, loopback, Air mode, USB-C bus power, and software including Ableton Live Lite, Pro Tools Intro+ and Cubase LE. Its spec page does not list MIDI.

What I look for: A clean, quiet recording of a piano starts with a quiet preamp and an honest headphone output. I look at how simple the front panel is: the fewer decisions between the piano and the file, the more often a student will actually record.

The one I recommend to a student who wants to record one microphone or the line out of a digital piano.

Pros
  • Simple, bus-powered USB-C unit
  • Phantom power for a condenser microphone
Cons
  • One microphone preamp, so a stereo pair of microphones does not fit
  • No MIDI listed on the specification page

Buy it if you record one microphone or the line out of a digital piano. Skip it if you want two microphones at once for a stereo piano recording.

Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 4th Gen

Who it is for: A pianist who wants two microphones for a stereo recording, or a microphone and a digital piano at the same time.

What the listing says: Focusrite lists two microphone preamps, two instrument inputs and two line inputs, two balanced line outputs, one headphone output, 24-bit/192 kHz converters, phantom power, loopback, Air mode, Auto Gain and Clip Safe, USB-C bus power, and the same software bundle as the Solo. Its spec page does not list MIDI.

What I look for: Two preamps is the feature that matters for piano: it lets you record in stereo with a matched pair. Auto Gain and Clip Safe help a beginner who sets levels too high on the loudest chord.

If you want to record a piano with two microphones, you need two preamps.

Pros
  • Two microphone preamps for stereo recording
  • Auto Gain and Clip Safe listed on the specification page
Cons
  • Larger than the Solo and more than a one-microphone student needs
  • No MIDI listed on the specification page

Buy it if you want a stereo recording or two sources. Skip it if you will only ever record one microphone.

What the listing says: The listing says a compact 2-in 2-out USB interface with a simple, clear layout.

What I look for: A layout that a beginner can read at a glance wins over a larger feature list.

A good choice for the beginner who wants things simple.

What I look for: MIDI connections matter if you also own a keyboard with MIDI ports.

Includes recording software and MIDI connections, per the listing.

How to choose

  • Count the microphones you will use: one preamp for one microphone, two for a stereo pair.
  • Check the connection: USB-C is common now.
  • Check whether it gives phantom power, which condenser microphones need.

Questions adults ask

Do I need an audio interface to record piano?

If you record a digital piano by USB you may not. For a microphone you need one.

How many inputs do I need?

One for a single microphone, two for a stereo pair.

What is phantom power?

A small voltage sent through the microphone cable that powers condenser microphones.
