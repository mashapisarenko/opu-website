Piano Bench Height: How to Sit and Choose a Bench

Sitting height changes your sound and your comfort. Here is the setup I teach, and the benches that make it easy.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 20, 2026

Why does piano bench height matter?

Height is the most common cause of the wrist and forearm trouble that adults describe to me, and the most common reason sound is harsh or weak. Get the bench right and the forearm, wrist and sound improve at once. A fixed chair takes the adjustment out of your hands entirely, which is why I do not recommend a dining chair.

How high should I sit at the piano?

This is the rule I teach: your elbows are level with the keys or slightly above them, so that your forearms are parallel to the floor, or slope very slightly down to the keys. Here is how to find it in your own room.

  1. Sit on the front half of the bench. Not the whole seat, and not leaning back. The front half lets your legs and feet move for the pedal and your torso stay free.
  2. Let your arms hang, then bend the elbows. Bring your hands up to the keys without lifting the shoulders.
  3. Look at your forearm. It should be parallel to the floor. If your elbows are below the keys, raise the bench. If your wrists are bent up and your forearms slope sharply down to the keys, lower the bench.
  4. Check the distance. Sit close enough that your arms have a slight bend at the elbow when you play in the middle of the keyboard, and that you can reach the pedal with your foot resting on the floor.
  5. Relax your shoulders. If they rise as you play, the bench is probably too low or too far.

Take a photo of yourself from the side. Seeing the forearm line is much more convincing than feeling it. If you record yourself, you can send me the video on the feedback page, and I will tell you what I see.

What if my bench is too low or too high?

If it is too low, add a firm, flat cushion or a folded towel rather than a soft one that compresses, and re-check the forearm line. If it is too high, lower it or choose an adjustable bench. A fixed bench is often right for one person and wrong for another, so in a shared household an adjustable one saves arguments.

Do I need a stand for a portable keyboard or digital piano?

A portable instrument needs a stable surface at the right height. A sturdy X-style stand such as the OnStage KT7800+ or the Liquid Stands piano keyboard stand is a common choice, and the Liquid Stands stand and bench set bundles both. Set the stand height first, then set the bench to match, since the keys are the fixed point and the bench is what you adjust. Roland lists a matching stand, the KSC-FP10, for the FP-10 (see it on Amazon).

What about foot position and the pedal?

Your feet should be comfortably placed so that your heel can rest on the floor while the toes work the pedal. If your feet dangle, the bench is too high. The foot moves after the hand, never with it, and I explain this in when to start using the pedal. Choose your pedal with the sustain pedal guide.

Questions adults ask

How high should my piano bench be?

High enough that your elbows are level with the keys or slightly above, with your forearms parallel to the floor.

How far should I sit from the piano?

Close enough that your arms keep a slight bend at the elbow in the middle of the keyboard and your foot reaches the pedal. Sit on the front half of the bench.

Do I need an adjustable piano bench?

It is the easiest way to get the height right. A fixed bench works only if its height happens to match your elbow height.

Can I use a chair instead of a piano bench?

A fixed chair takes away the height adjustment and often has arms or a back that stop you moving. An adjustable bench is better.

Why do my wrists hurt when I play?

Bench height is a common cause. Check that your forearms are parallel to the floor and that you are not reaching. If pain continues, stop and speak to a medical professional.
