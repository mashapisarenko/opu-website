Piano Music for the Last Night of the Year

A concert pianist suggests real piano music for New Year's Eve, from Chopin and Brahms waltzes to Strauss, and explains how to play something yourself at midnight.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  December 31, 2026

Taught by Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA — the complete beginner course is free: Start at Lesson 1 →

The last night of the year has its own music. In many countries the waltz belongs to it: in Vienna, the New Year’s Concert on the first of January traditionally includes the waltzes of Johann Strauss II. Here is music worth hearing on New Year’s Eve, and a plan for playing something yourself when the year turns.

Waltzes to listen to

Chopin, Waltz in D-flat major, Op. 64 No. 1. Often called the “Minute” Waltz, though it was never meant to be played in a minute. The nickname means “small.” Listen for how light the right hand stays as it circles, and how the left hand keeps the dance steady underneath.

Chopin, Waltz in C-sharp minor, Op. 64 No. 2. A more inward waltz, with a sense of looking back that suits the end of a year. Listen to the way the pianist bends the tempo slightly in the melody while the bass keeps its place.

Chopin, Grande valse brillante in E-flat major, Op. 18. This is a ballroom waltz, festive and bright. It opens with a fanfare of repeated notes and then moves through one dance after another. Listen to how each new section brings a change of mood.

Brahms, Waltz in A-flat major, Op. 39 No. 15. One of the best-known short piano pieces Brahms wrote. It is gentle and warm, more lullaby than ballroom. Listen to the inner voices. Brahms fills the middle of the keyboard with harmony, and a good pianist lets it glow without covering the melody.

Tchaikovsky, “December” from The Seasons. The last piece in Tchaikovsky’s cycle of the twelve months is itself a waltz, written for the Christmas season. It is a fitting close to the year in music.

Johann Strauss II, The Blue Danube. Written for orchestra and chorus, but it has been arranged for piano many times, in versions from very simple to very difficult. Listen to the long introduction, which seems to wake up slowly before the waltz begins.

What makes a waltz sound like a waltz

A waltz is in three, but it is not three equal beats. The bass note on the first beat is deep and light; the two chords that follow are softer still and shorter. The melody floats above. When adults play waltzes, the most common problem is a heavy chord on every beat, which turns a dance into a march. As you listen tonight, notice how little weight the accompaniment carries in a fine performance. When you play a waltz yourself, practice the left hand alone first, and make the two chords after the bass almost a whisper.

Playing something yourself at midnight

There is a quiet pleasure in playing the first music of the new year with your own hands. It does not need to be difficult. It needs to be ready.

  • Choose something short and familiar. A piece you already play securely, or a simple arrangement of “Auld Lang Syne,” the old Scottish song that many people sing at midnight. A melody in the right hand with single bass notes in the left is enough.
  • Practice playing it cold. At midnight you will not have warmed up. For the week before, play the piece once at the start of each session, without preparation. That is the real test.
  • Play it softer than you think. Late at night, and for people talking or listening, a gentle sound carries better than a loud one. It also hides small slips.
  • Decide the tempo before you start. Sing the first line in your head, then begin at that speed. Nerves make everyone rush.
  • Begin with stillness. Place your hands, take a breath, wait two seconds, then play. The silence helps you, and it helps your listeners.

A recording to start the year

If you are alone at the piano, record what you play. That recording becomes the first entry of your new year. Next December you can listen to it and hear, with your own ears, how far you have come. For an adult learning without a teacher, that small file may be the most honest gift you give yourself tonight.
