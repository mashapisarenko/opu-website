A Five-Minute Warm-Up That Is Actually Worth Doing

A warm-up is not practice and it is not an exercise. Its whole job is to get the hand and the ear ready, and that takes five minutes.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  January 13, 2027

The warm-up is taught in Lesson 1 of the free course, before any note: Start the free course →

Adults skip the warm-up, and they are half right to. Most of what is printed under that heading is finger exercises — useful or not, but not a warm-up. An exercise asks the hand to work. A warm-up asks it to wake up.

The difference matters, because a cold hand grips, and a gripping hand cannot hear. The first ten minutes of your practice are worth roughly half as much when you start cold, so five minutes spent properly buys back more than it costs.

Before the keyboard: thirty seconds

Stand up. Let both arms hang at your sides, completely. Shake the hands loosely, as though flicking water off them, five or six times. Roll the shoulders back twice.

Then make tight fists and snap the fingers open, wide, five times. You are asking the small muscles to notice they exist.

Sit down and check the bench: forearms level with the keys or a little above, feet flat, elbows with room in front of you.

One: single notes, listening (one minute)

Play one note. Just one. Let the arm drop into it rather than pressing from the finger, and listen to the whole sound — the attack, and then the long decay after it.

Do that eight or ten times, moving up the keyboard, alternating hands. It sounds like nothing and it does two things at once: it sets the arm weight for the session, and it turns your ear on before your fingers get busy.

Two: five notes, very slowly (two minutes)

Right hand thumb on C, one finger per key: C D E F G and back down. Slowly. What you are listening for is evenness — are all five notes the same weight? Most people have one finger that speaks louder (usually the thumb) and one that fades (usually the fourth).

Then the same with the left hand. Then, if you are comfortable hands together, both.

Do not speed it up. Speed is not the point, and this is not an exercise — you are checking that the hand does what you ask it to.

Three: something you know (a minute and a half)

Play a few bars of a piece you finished weeks ago, easily, at a comfortable tempo. It moves the hand into real music rather than patterns, and it puts you in a good mood, which is a legitimate goal.

The check that ends it

One last thing, and it takes five seconds. Put your hands in your lap and notice your shoulders. If they are up near your ears — and after a working day they usually are — drop them, breathe out, and begin.

Five minutes. No exercise book, nothing to buy, nothing to get better at. The hand is warm, the ear is on, the shoulders are down, and the practice that follows is worth more than it would have been.
