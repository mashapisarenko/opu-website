What Should Actually Happen in a Piano Lesson

Most adults have never seen a good lesson and so have nothing to compare theirs with. Here is what the hour is supposed to contain.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  October 26, 2026

The free course is built on the same structure, lesson by lesson: Open the free course →

Adults are strikingly bad at judging their own lessons, and it is not their fault: they have nothing to compare against. A lesson where the teacher is friendly and the hour passes pleasantly feels like a good lesson. Sometimes it is. Often it is an hour of supervised practice, which is a different and much less valuable thing.

So here is what I think the hour should contain. Not a rigid order — a set of things that should all be somewhere in it.

1. Something is diagnosed

The teacher should, at some point, tell you what is wrong. Specifically: not “that was a bit uneven” but the fourth finger is arriving late because the hand is turning toward the thumb, and here is where you can hear it.

A diagnosis has three parts — what is happening, why, and where in the music you can hear it. If you leave your lessons knowing that things could be better but not what specifically to change, you are being assessed rather than taught, and that is the commonest failure in the trade.

2. Something is fixed in the room

Not assigned to be fixed at home. Fixed, while the teacher is watching, so that you have physically felt the correct version at least once.

This matters because you cannot reproduce at home a sensation you have never had. A student sent away to “work on evenness” will practice their existing habit more thoroughly. A student who has felt the even version four times under supervision has something to aim at.

3. You are told how to practice it, not just what to practice

“Practice bars 9 to 16” is not an instruction, it is a location. The instruction is the method: hands separately at this speed, then this rhythm variant, then together but only the first half, and here is how you will know it has worked.

Ask for this explicitly if it is not offered. It is the difference between a week of useful practice and a week of repetition.

4. You play something all the way through

A lesson consisting entirely of corrected fragments produces a student who can play no piece from start to finish. Some part of the hour should be uninterrupted playing, with the teacher listening and saying nothing until the end.

This is also the only way either of you finds out whether the piece actually holds together.

5. Something is heard, not just played

Somewhere in the hour the teacher should play, and you should listen — a phrase, a demonstration of two ways of doing the same bar. Description has limits. A sound has none.

This is the part most commonly missing in online teaching, and it is worth asking for.

6. You leave knowing the one thing

If you are given nine corrections you will carry out none. A good lesson ends with one priority, said plainly, that everything else waits behind.

If your teacher does not do this, do it yourself on the way home: decide which single thing you are taking. You will make more progress on one than on nine.

Signs the lesson is not working

You play, the teacher says “good, now the next one”, repeatedly. That is an audit of your homework, not teaching.

The same fault is mentioned every week for two months. Mentioning is not fixing. If it has not moved, the approach needs to change — and that is the teacher's job to notice, not yours.

You are never told why. Adults learn through understanding; a teacher who will not explain the mechanism is teaching you as though you were seven.

Nothing is ever taken away from you. Good teaching removes things — a piece that is too hard, a habit, an unnecessary movement. A lesson that only adds material is not being steered.

What is reasonable to ask for

All of this is reasonable to ask for directly, and most teachers will respond well to being asked. A useful sentence: what is the one thing you want me to change this week, and how will I know when I have?

If you are learning alone, you are your own teacher for all six of these, which is harder but not impossible — the six give you a checklist for your own practice.
