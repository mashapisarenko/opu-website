Best Sustain Pedal for Digital Piano: What to Buy

The pedal in the box is only a start. Here is how to choose a pedal that teaches the foot properly.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 19, 2026

Forty-one lessons in order, free, at your own pace: Start the free course →

The best sustain pedal for a digital piano is a piano-style pedal that your instrument supports. For Yamaha, look at the Yamaha FC4A; for Roland, the DP-10 with its continuous/switch selector. Check your piano's manual for compatibility before buying.

My pick

Yamaha FC4A piano-style foot switch

Yamaha describes it as a piano-style foot switch for sustain and other momentary (on/off) functions. It has a 1/4-inch TS plug. It is a big step up in feel from the small footswitch in many boxes.

Why do I need a better sustain pedal?

The small footswitch in the box works electrically and is poor musically: it slides on the floor, it has little travel and it teaches the foot nothing. A piano-style pedal sits firmly, has a real shape and teaches your foot the correct movement from the start, so you will not have to relearn it on an acoustic piano.

When should I start using the pedal?

Later than most adults expect, and then with discipline. The foot moves after the hand, never with it. I explain the order of lessons in when should you start using the pedal. Whatever you buy, get the habit right from the first day.

Will any pedal work with my piano?

No, and this is where adults waste money. Pianos differ in connector and polarity, and in whether they respond to partial pedaling. Yamaha lists a half pedal as available with the optional FC3A on the P-45, supports the optional LP-5A three-pedal unit on the P-145BT, and lists a pedal unit (LP-1, FC35) as optional for the P-225. Roland lists the DP-10 as sold separately for the FP-10 and optional for the FP-30X, which also has the KPD-70 pedal unit as an option. Read your manual and buy what it names.

How should my foot move on the pedal?

The foot moves after the hand, never with it. Play the chord or note, then press the pedal; lift it as the next harmony begins, and press again at once. Keep your heel on the floor and let the toes do the work, and the movement is small and quiet. A pedal that slides, or a bench that is too high, makes this impossible, which is why those two purchases belong together. Practice without sound first: watch your foot, and listen only to the pedal's mechanism, before you add the music.

What is half-pedaling and do beginners need it?

Half-pedaling means partly lifting the pedal so the sound is cleaned but not cut off. It is a refinement of the intermediate level. A beginner needs a pedal that is reliable and piano-like first. If your keyboard supports continuous pedals, a pedal such as the DP-10 lets you learn it when you are ready.

How do I stop a pedal from sliding?

Choose one with a non-slip base; Roland lists a rubber plate for the DP-10. Place the pedal where your foot rests naturally, with your heel on the floor, and sit properly: see how to choose a piano bench and sit at the right height.

How do I set up a new pedal?

Plug it into the correct jack before you switch the piano on, because some instruments read the pedal's polarity at start-up. Press the pedal and play a note: the sound should hold until you lift. If it behaves backwards, switch the piano off, check that the plug is fully in, and turn it on again without touching the pedal. If it still holds when it should not, the polarity may not match, and your manual or the maker's support page is the place to check. Then put it where your foot lands naturally, on a non-slip surface.

What pianos pair with these pedals?

The Yamaha P-145BT and the Yamaha P-225 ship with a footswitch; the Roland FP-30X ships with a pedal switch. See Yamaha P-145 vs P-225 and Roland FP-30X vs Yamaha P-225.

Questions adults ask

What is the best sustain pedal for a digital piano?

A piano-style pedal that your instrument supports: the Yamaha FC4A for Yamaha, the Roland DP-10 for Roland. Check your manual first.

Will any sustain pedal work with my keyboard?

Not always. Connectors and polarity differ, and not all pianos respond to half-pedaling. Buy the pedal your manual names.

Do I need a sustain pedal as a beginner?

You will need one soon. Start using it after the first lessons, and learn the foot-after-hand movement properly.

What is the difference between a footswitch and a piano-style pedal?

A piano-style pedal is shaped and weighted like a piano pedal, so the foot learns the right movement. A small footswitch slides and has little travel.

What is a half pedal?

Partly lifting the pedal to clean the sound without cutting it off. It requires a pedal and a piano that support continuous control.
