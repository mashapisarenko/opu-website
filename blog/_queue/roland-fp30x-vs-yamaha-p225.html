Roland FP-30X vs Yamaha P-225: Which Is Better?

Two of the most popular portable digital pianos, side by side, with the numbers from the makers' own pages.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 17, 2026

The Roland FP-30X has stronger specifications on paper: 256-note polyphony and 11 W x 2 speakers, against the Yamaha P-225's 192 and 7 W x 2 amplifiers. The P-225 is lighter. Both have 88 keys. Choose by key feel: try both if you can.

My pick

Roland FP-30X

Roland lists an 88-key PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and Ivory Feel, 256-note maximum polyphony, 11 W x 2 speakers, Bluetooth audio and two headphone jacks. A fuller sound and a heavier body.

The sizes are quoted under different conditions, so do not compare them to the millimetre: Roland lists 1,300 x 284 x 151 mm with the music rest detached, and Yamaha lists 1,326 mm wide, 129 mm high and 272 mm deep (327 mm high and 362 mm deep with the music rest).

Which has the better keys?

That is the question that matters, and the table cannot answer it. Both are graded, weighted actions. Roland's PHA-4 Standard adds escapement, the subtle notch you feel at the bottom of a slow keypress on an acoustic grand, and Ivory Feel keytops. Yamaha's GHC is the keyboard Yamaha lists on both the P-145BT and the P-225. If you can press both in a shop, play one note very softly and one loudly, and play a quiet repeated note. Choose the one your hand wants to practice on every day. Read weighted vs semi-weighted keys for background.

Which sounds better?

On paper the FP-30X has bigger speakers and more polyphony. In practice most adults practice on headphones, so what matters is how the piano sounds in your ears. I recommend listening on headphones, and my picks are in the best headphones for digital piano. With either, the Sennheiser HD 280 Pro or the Audio-Technica ATH-M40x are comfortable closed-back pairs.

Which is better for a beginner?

Roland FP-30X

Who it is for: The student who wants a fuller speaker sound, a Roland key feel and generous polyphony.

The heavier of the two at 14.8 kg with the music rest. Roland lists the DP-10 damper pedal and a KPD-70 pedal unit as optional; the Roland DP-10 is the pedal I would pair with it.

Yamaha P-225

Who it is for: The student who wants a lighter piano that is easy to move.

At 11.5 kg it is easier to carry. Yamaha lists 24 voices and a 24-bit USB audio interface. Add a piano-style pedal.

How do I decide in a shop?

Bring a short piece you know well, or a scale, and play it on both pianos at the same volume through headphones. Listen for three things. Does a soft note stay soft and even when you repeat it quickly? Does the loud end of the keyboard answer without harshness? Do your weakest fingers, 4 and 5, feel in control? Spend a minute on each question before you look at the speaker or the price. The piano whose keys make you want to keep playing is the one to buy. If you cannot visit a shop, check the return terms on the day and treat the first week as a trial.

Which pedal works with each?

Roland lists the DP-10 as an optional damper pedal for the FP-30X, and the DP-10 has a continuous/switch selector. Yamaha lists a pedal unit among the P-225's optional accessories and a footswitch in the box. Always check your instrument's manual for the pedal it supports; see the sustain pedal guide and the pedals page.

Which is easier to live with day to day?

The P-225 is lighter at 11.5 kg versus Roland's 14.8 kg, so if the piano needs to move between rooms, Yamaha has the advantage. If it stays in one place, the weight of the FP-30X does not matter and its larger speakers may. Both need a stable stand and a bench at the right height, and both are comfortable on headphones. Bluetooth audio is useful on both for playing along with a recording.

Should I consider the cheaper siblings?

If the price gap is large on the day, the Roland FP-10 shares the FP-30X's PHA-4 Standard keyboard, and the Yamaha P-145BT shares the P-225's keyboard type. Either is a sensible way to get the key action you want in a lower tier. See the digital pianos page.

What else should I buy?

A bench at the right height (how to choose), and a stand or a matching accessory from the benches and stands page. For the broader picture, the best digital piano for adult beginners places both in context.

Questions adults ask

Is the Roland FP-30X better than the Yamaha P-225?

On paper, the FP-30X has more polyphony and louder speakers; the P-225 is lighter. Which is better depends on which key feel you prefer.

Which is lighter, the FP-30X or the P-225?

Yamaha lists the P-225 at 11.5 kg; Roland lists the FP-30X at 14.8 kg with the music rest.

Do both have Bluetooth?

Roland lists Bluetooth audio and MIDI on the FP-30X; Yamaha lists Bluetooth audio on the P-225, noting that it varies by area.

How many headphone jacks do they have?

Both are listed with two headphone jacks.

Which is better for a beginner?

Both are good. Try the keys if you can. If not, choose by weight and budget tier, and add a proper pedal and bench.
