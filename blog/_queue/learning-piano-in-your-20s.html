Learning Piano in Your 20s: Beyond Falling-Bar Tutorials

You can learn almost anything from a screen. Piano is the one skill where the screen can quietly teach your hands the wrong thing. Here is how to keep the speed and lose the habits.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 15, 2026

Forty-one free lessons, in the right order: Start at Lesson 1 →

If you are in your twenties, you have probably learned a song from a video where colored bars fall onto a keyboard. That is a perfectly good way to get your first piece under your fingers. It also has three limits that nobody mentions.

Limit one: you copy, you do not read

Each falling-bar song is a separate project. You cannot take what you learned from River Flows in You and use it to learn Merry-Go-Round of Life faster, because you never learned what the notes are. Reading music is the skill that turns every new piece from a project into a variation. It takes weeks, not years, when it is taught in the right order.

Limit two: the hand learns the wrong shape

Playing on a small keyboard on a desk, with the wrists low and the shoulders lifted, feels fine for a month. Then the forearm starts to complain. The hand should rest its weight into the key, curved and relaxed, with the wrist level. That is the very first thing I teach, before any notes.

Limit three: you want the beautiful pieces, and they need specific skills

The music people in their twenties most often want to play — Joe Hisaishi's Ghibli themes, Zelda's Lullaby, Hans Zimmer's Cornfield Chase, Einaudi, Yiruma — is built on flowing left-hand patterns, the pedal and a singing melody on top. Those are learnable skills. Practiced in order, they bring the pieces within reach far sooner than trying each piece from scratch.

What to keep from the videos

The speed and the motivation. Keep learning songs you love; that energy is valuable. Just add, alongside it, twenty minutes a day of the foundations: reading, hand position, one new skill at a time.

The page for adults in their 20s lists the pieces people your age most want to play, from Ghibli and game themes to Billie Eilish and Clair de Lune. Tell me which one you want, and I will point you to the lessons that lead to it.
