Digital Piano vs Keyboard: What to Buy to Learn

The difference that matters is under your fingers. Here is how to choose between a digital piano and a keyboard.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 12, 2026

Forty-one lessons in order, free, at your own pace: Start the free course →

A digital piano has weighted keys that resist your fingers like an acoustic piano; a keyboard usually has lighter, springier keys and more sounds. For learning piano, choose the digital piano. Choose a keyboard only for small budgets, tight spaces, travel or a short trial.

What is the difference between a digital piano and a keyboard?

The difference that matters is the key. A digital piano is built to imitate the feel of an acoustic piano: weighted keys, a graded action and a piano sound first. A keyboard is built to offer variety: many sounds, rhythms and learning functions, usually with lighter keys. Both make sound without strings, and both can have headphones, but they train the hand differently.

Everything I teach in the first year depends on the key pushing back: arm weight, a firm fingertip, soft and loud. For the detail, see weighted versus semi-weighted keys.

Which is better for learning piano?

The digital piano, and it is not close. A hand trained on light keys has not yet learned the movement a piano asks for, so the first time the student meets a real piano, the pieces they know do not come out. Learning on a weighted instrument means what your hand learns is what a piano needs. If you can only spend a small amount, a weighted 88-key digital piano in the starter tier still beats a feature-heavy keyboard.

When does a keyboard make sense?

When space, travel or cost leaves no room. The Casio CT-S1 is the one I would look at: Casio lists 61 keys, 3 touch-sensitivity levels (or off), a weight of 4.5 kg without batteries and a headphone/output jack. It can run on six AA batteries or the AC adaptor. A keyboard is a way to start reading music and finding notes while you save for a piano. Plan on moving to a weighted instrument within the first year. My keyboard picks are in the best keyboard for beginners, and the keyboards page has more.

Do digital pianos feel like real pianos?

Close enough to learn on, and not identical. No digital action fully reproduces an acoustic piano, but a graded, weighted action gets the essentials right: the bass keys are heavier, the treble keys lighter, and the key returns under your finger. Yamaha names its actions GHC and GHS, and Roland names its action PHA-4 Standard with escapement. When you are choosing, the practical test is simple: play one note very softly and one note loudly, and listen for a clear difference.

Does the sound or the speaker matter?

Less than the keys, and more than most beginners think. Speakers vary between models; Roland lists 6 W x 2 speakers for the FP-10 and Yamaha lists 7 W x 2 amplifiers for the P-145BT. In practice most adults play on headphones, so the headphone jack and a comfortable pair of headphones deserve as much thought as the speakers.

Questions adults ask

Is a digital piano better than a keyboard for beginners?

Yes, for learning piano. Weighted keys teach touch and technique that a lighter keyboard cannot. A keyboard is a workable start only when space, travel or cost requires it.

Can I learn piano on a keyboard?

You can learn note names, rhythm and reading, but not the touch of a piano. Plan to move to a weighted instrument within the first year.

What is the difference between a digital piano and a synthesizer?

A digital piano focuses on a piano sound and a piano-like key feel. A synthesizer focuses on creating and shaping sounds, and usually does not try to imitate piano keys.

How many keys do I need?

Eighty-eight keys is the full piano range. Sixty-one keys is a real limit that you will meet inside your first year. See my 61 vs 76 vs 88 keys guide.

Is a digital piano worth it if I might quit?

A starter-tier weighted digital piano is a sensible way to find out, and it keeps its use for a long time. The free 41-lesson course is a no-cost way to test your commitment first.
