How to Run Your Own Piano Lesson When You Do Not Have a Teacher

Most of what a teacher does in an hour, you can do for yourself once you know what the hour is supposed to contain. One part you cannot, and it is worth knowing which.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  October 26, 2026

The free course is structured the same way, lesson by lesson: Open the free course →

Adults teaching themselves usually practice. They rarely have a lesson. Those are different activities, and running the second one for yourself — even once a week, even for twenty minutes — changes how fast the first one works.

A lesson is not more practice. It is the hour where you step back, work out what is actually wrong, fix one thing, and decide what the week is for. Here is what a good one contains, and how to run each part alone.

1. Diagnose something — specifically

Not “bar twelve is messy”. A diagnosis has three parts: what is happening, why, and where you can hear it.

Alone: record ninety seconds and listen back with a pencil. Write down the first three things you hear, in that three-part form. Writing them down is what forces the specificity — listening without a pencil produces a general feeling of dissatisfaction, which is useless.

Then pick one. Not three.

2. Fix it now, not “this week”

The point of a lesson is to feel the correct version at least once while you are paying attention, so that you have something to aim at. If you leave with only an intention, you will practice your existing habit more thoroughly.

Alone: take the one thing, slow it down until you can play it right, and play it right four times in a row. Slow enough is whatever speed makes it correct — if that is absurdly slow, that is the information, not a failure.

3. Decide the method, not just the place

“Work on bars 9 to 16” is a location. The instruction is: hands separately at this speed, then this rhythm variant, then together but only the first half, and here is how I will know it worked.

Alone: write the week's method on one line and leave it on the music desk. A week of repetition and a week of practice look identical from the outside and produce very different results.

4. Play something all the way through, uninterrupted

A week made entirely of corrected fragments produces someone who can play no piece from beginning to end. Self-teachers are especially prone to this, because nobody is there to make them commit.

Alone: once per session, play one piece start to finish with no stopping, whatever happens. Mistakes included. This is also the only way you find out whether it holds together.

5. Hear it done, not just described

Words have limits. A sound has none, and this is the part a book genuinely cannot give you.

Alone: find a recording of what you are learning — a real one, by a pianist — and listen once before you play and once after. Not to copy it, to know what the piece is supposed to be. Adults working from a book often have no idea what they are aiming at, and then wonder why it does not arrive.

6. Leave with one thing

Nine corrections produce none. End by saying one sentence out loud: this week is about keeping the left hand under the right. That is the whole assignment.

The part you cannot do alone

Five of those six work perfectly well by yourself. The first one does not, entirely, and it is worth being honest about why.

You can hear that something is wrong. You usually cannot hear what, because the fault is generally in the thing you are not attending to — and you are not attending to it precisely because you do not know it is there. A raised shoulder, a wrist that drops on every fourth beat, a fourth finger arriving late: these are invisible from inside. That is the one genuine function of a teacher that a book and a video cannot replace.

Two things narrow the gap considerably. Film your hands, not just the sound — from the side, at keyboard height, where the wrist is visible. You will see things you cannot feel. And let someone experienced listen occasionally. It does not have to be weekly; it has to be sometimes, because a fault caught in month two takes a week to fix and the same fault caught in year two takes months.
