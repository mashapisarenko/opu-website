Why You Cannot Play Fast Yet (It Is Not Your Fingers)

Adults assume speed comes with strength or youth. It comes from efficiency of movement, and here is what is eating yours.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  January 20, 2027

You can play the passage. You cannot play it at the speed it is meant to go. Push the tempo and it collapses in the same place every time.

Nearly every adult concludes their fingers are too slow, or too old, or not strong enough. That is almost never what is happening. Piano keys need very little force, and the finger movements involved are small and quick for essentially every adult hand. Something else is costing you the time, and it is usually one of four things.

1. You are still deciding

The most common cause by far. At slow speed you have time to work out the next note, the next fingering, the next hand position — and you are doing exactly that, invisibly, every time you play it.

Speed removes that time. If any part of the passage is still being decided rather than known, that is where it breaks.

The test: can you play the passage while saying something out loud — counting, or naming the beats? If your speech stops where the passage falters, you were thinking, not playing.

The fix: fix the fingering in writing and never change it again, and practice the passage until it survives that talking test. Speed usually arrives on its own afterward.

2. You are lifting too far

Watch your fingers in a fast passage. If they rise well clear of the keys between notes, every note is a round trip — up and back down — and at speed there is no time for the journey.

Fast playing happens close to the keys. The finger stays near the surface and the movement is small. This feels unnatural at first because high lifting feels like effort, and effort feels like work.

The fix: practice the passage at medium speed with the fingers deliberately touching the key surface between notes. It will feel lazy. It is the correct motion.

3. You are holding on

Tension is the real speed limit. A hand that is gripping has to overcome itself for every note, and fast passages are where that tax becomes unpayable.

Check the usual suspects: shoulders up, elbows clamped to the ribs, thumb pressing into the key it is not playing, jaw tight, breath held. Any one of them will cap your tempo.

The fix: play the passage at half speed, and once per bar deliberately release — drop the shoulders, let the wrist float, exhale. You are training the hand to stay loose at speed, and it has to learn that slowly.

4. You practiced it fast too early

If you have been playing it fast and messy for weeks, you have been rehearsing the mess. The hand learned a version of the passage that includes the stumble, and now the stumble is part of what it knows.

The fix is the only slow one here. Take it right back to a speed at which it is completely clean, live there for a few days, and come up in small steps from accuracy rather than from ambition.

The one that is not on the list

Finger strength. Building it will not increase your speed, and the exercises sold for it tend to add tension, which reduces it. Speed is an efficiency problem, not a power problem — which is good news, because efficiency is trainable at any age.
