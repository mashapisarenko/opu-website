Audient EVO 4 vs Focusrite Scarlett Solo: Which Interface?

Two small USB interfaces. The key difference for a pianist is how many microphones each can record at once.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  February 6, 2027

Shopping for this? My picks, chosen by a concert pianist: Audio Interfaces and Microphones and Interfaces.

The difference that matters for a pianist is the number of microphone preamps. Audient lists two on the EVO 4, so a stereo pair of microphones fits. Focusrite lists one on the Scarlett Solo, which is enough for a digital piano's line output or a single microphone, but not for a stereo pair. If you may ever record an acoustic piano in stereo, choose the EVO 4; if you will only record a digital piano or your voice, the Solo is enough.

My top 3 picks

  • For a stereo pair: Audient EVO 4 — two microphone preamps
  • For a digital piano or one microphone: Focusrite Scarlett Solo 4th Gen — one preamp, one instrument and one line input
  • If you want a third option: Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 4th Gen — two preamps, plus Auto Gain and Clip Safe

How I choose

I compare what each interface lets you plug in, because that decides what you can record. Sound quality at this level matters less than whether the box can do what you need. I have not played every instrument or used every device in this guide, and I do not say that I have. What I give you is what I look for as a concert pianist who teaches adults, checked against what each Amazon listing and manufacturer page actually states.

What this means for a pianist

If you own a digital piano, the Solo's line input takes the piano's stereo output and gives you a clean recording and a clear headphone output. If you own an acoustic piano and want to record it, a pair of microphones needs two preamps, which is the EVO 4. Both interfaces run from the computer's USB cable, so there is no power brick to find. Audient says SmartGain sets the level on both inputs for you, and Focusrite lists Air mode; I cannot tell you how either sounds, and I would not choose on a feature name. Read the other guides if you are still deciding: the best audio interface for beginners, Scarlett Solo vs 2i2 and do I need an audio interface for a digital piano?.

Audient EVO 4

Who it is for: A beginner who wants two microphone inputs, so a stereo pair of microphones fits, in a small bus-powered box.

What the listing says: Audient lists two microphone preamps, two inputs of which one is a JFET instrument input, two outputs, a headphone output, SmartGain, loopback, USB bus power and a USB-C connection. Its tech-spec page lists 48 V phantom power, a 360 g unit and a 1 m USB-C to USB-A cable in the box. I did not find MIDI on the pages I read.

What I look for: A layout I can read at a glance, and two microphone preamps. Two preamps are what let me recommend a stereo pair of microphones on a piano, which a one-preamp box cannot do.

My choice if there is any chance you will record an acoustic piano with two microphones.

Pros
  • Two microphone preamps, so a stereo pair fits
  • SmartGain sets the input levels for you, which helps a beginner
Cons
  • Audient's overview page does not state sample rate or bit depth
  • I did not find MIDI on the pages I read

Buy it if you may want to record a piano with two microphones. Skip it if you will only ever record one microphone or one cable from a piano.

Focusrite Scarlett Solo 4th Gen

Who it is for: A pianist who records one microphone, or the stereo line from a digital piano, and wants a simple box.

What the listing says: Focusrite lists one microphone preamp, one instrument input and one line input, two balanced line outputs, one headphone output, 24-bit/192 kHz converters, phantom power, loopback, Air mode, USB-C bus power, and software including Ableton Live Lite, Pro Tools Intro+ and Cubase LE. Its spec page does not list MIDI.

What I look for: A clean, quiet recording of a piano starts with a quiet preamp and an honest headphone output. I look at how simple the front panel is: the fewer decisions between the piano and the file, the more often a student will actually record.

My choice if you will record a digital piano or a single microphone.

Pros
  • Simple, bus-powered USB-C unit
  • Phantom power for a condenser microphone
Cons
  • One microphone preamp, so a stereo pair of microphones does not fit
  • No MIDI listed on the specification page

Buy it if you record one microphone or the line out of a digital piano. Skip it if you want two microphones at once for a stereo piano recording.

Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 4th Gen

Who it is for: A pianist who wants two microphones for a stereo recording, or a microphone and a digital piano at the same time.

What the listing says: Focusrite lists two microphone preamps, two instrument inputs and two line inputs, two balanced line outputs, one headphone output, 24-bit/192 kHz converters, phantom power, loopback, Air mode, Auto Gain and Clip Safe, USB-C bus power, and the same software bundle as the Solo. Its spec page does not list MIDI.

What I look for: Two preamps is the feature that matters for piano: it lets you record in stereo with a matched pair. Auto Gain and Clip Safe help a beginner who sets levels too high on the loudest chord.

The Focusrite model with two preamps, if you prefer that maker and want a stereo pair.

Pros
  • Two microphone preamps for stereo recording
  • Auto Gain and Clip Safe listed on the specification page
Cons
  • Larger than the Solo and more than a one-microphone student needs
  • No MIDI listed on the specification page

Buy it if you want a stereo recording or two sources. Skip it if you will only ever record one microphone.

Questions adults ask

How many microphones can the EVO 4 record at once?

Audient lists two microphone preamps on the EVO 4, so two.

Is the Scarlett Solo enough for a digital piano?

Yes. It has an instrument input and a line input, which suit a digital piano's output.

Which is better for a beginner?

Either is simple. The EVO 4 gives you room to grow; the Solo is the simplest box for one source.
