Roland FP-10 Review: A Piano Teacher's Assessment (2026)

What Roland says

Roland lists 88 keys on the PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and ivory-feel white keys, five key touch types plus a fixed option, 96-voice polyphony, 36 tones (15 selectable on the front panel), two 12 cm speakers rated at 6 W each, a single headphone jack, a pedal switch in the box and a weight of 12.6 kg with the music rest detached. Bluetooth MIDI is listed, though Roland notes it may not be included depending on your country of purchase.

What I look for, and how the FP-10 answers

  • Graded action. Heavier keys in the bass, lighter in the treble, as on an acoustic piano. The PHA-4 Standard is Roland's keyboard for this.
  • Escapement. A small notch you feel when the key releases the hammer, helpful for soft playing and repeated notes.
  • Polyphony. At 96 voices, the piano can run out when you hold the pedal through a long passage. For first-year music it is enough.
  • Headphone jack. One, so a teacher cannot listen alongside you; use the speakers or a splitter if you need it.
  • Pedal. A switch comes with it; I would add a proper pedal. See the best sustain pedals.

Roland FP-10

Who it is for: An adult beginner in a small room who wants a proper piano action and nothing extra.

What the listing says: Roland lists 88 keys on its PHA-4 Standard keyboard with escapement and ivory-feel white keys, 96-voice polyphony, two 12 cm speakers, one headphone jack, a pedal switch in the box and a weight of 12.6 kg without the music rest.

What I look for: Whether the keys feel graded (heavier in the bass, lighter in the treble), whether quiet notes sound at the touch you meant, and whether a repeated note answers quickly. Escapement, the small notch you feel as the key lets go of the hammer, is the first thing I check on a beginner piano because it teaches control of soft playing.

I would recommend it to the student who asks me for one piano that feels right and does not need to do everything.

Pros
  • Real graded action in a compact, light body
  • Simple controls that do not distract a beginner
Cons
  • 96-voice polyphony runs out sooner with long pedaled passages
  • One headphone jack, so a teacher cannot listen alongside you

Buy it if you want the best key feel for a modest budget and a small footprint. Skip it if you already know you will play pedal-heavy Romantic pieces, or you want a second headphone jack.
