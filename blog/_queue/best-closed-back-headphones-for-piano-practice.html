Best Closed-Back Headphones for Piano Practice (2027)

Closed-back headphones keep the sound in and the room out. Here is why I prefer them for practice, and three I would look at.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  February 3, 2027

Shopping for this? My picks, chosen by a concert pianist: Headphones and Piano Accessories.

For piano practice I recommend closed-back headphones, because they keep the sound of the piano in your ears and out of the room, and they keep the room out of your ears. The three I would look at first are the Sennheiser HD 280 Pro, the Audio-Technica ATH-M50x and the beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro X. This is a pianist's assessment of what the listings say, not a hands-on test.

My top 3 picks

  • Quiet and plain: Sennheiser HD 280 Pro — closed-back studio headphones
  • A well-known classic: Audio-Technica ATH-M50x — closed-back studio monitor headphones
  • Easy to drive: beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro X (48 Ohm) — closed-back studio headphones rated at 48 ohms

How I choose

Headphones are what you hear your own touch through, so I look at comfort first, then whether the middle of the sound is clear, then whether the bass is steady, and last at whether the leakage is low. I have not played every instrument or used every device in this guide, and I do not say that I have. What I give you is what I look for as a concert pianist who teaches adults, checked against what each Amazon listing and manufacturer page actually states.

Why closed-back?

Open-back headphones let sound pass through the earcups. They can sound airy, but they let the sound out and the room in, which defeats the purpose if you practice at night, in an apartment, or with other people in the house. Closed-back headphones seal the sound in. On a digital piano, that means the piano is the only thing you hear, and nobody else hears it. If you live in a small space, read how to practice piano in an apartment.

What I look for

  • Comfort for an hour. You will wear them for as long as a lesson.
  • A clear middle. A piano's melody lives there. Headphones that scoop the middle make the melody harder to follow.
  • Steady bass. Boomy bass makes you play the left hand too softly.
  • Whether your piano can drive them. A high-impedance pair can sound quiet from a piano's headphone jack. A lower figure, such as the 48 ohms in the DT 770 Pro X listing, is easier to drive.
  • A cable that is long enough to reach the piano from your bench.

A note on adapter plugs

Many digital pianos have a quarter-inch jack and many headphones have a small plug with an adapter, or the other way round. Check what is in the box. For the longer list, see the best headphones for digital piano and the headphones page in my shop.

What about the lower-cost pair?

The Audio-Technica ATH-M20x is a closed-back pair at a lower cost. Audio-Technica's own page, which I opened on November 16, 2026, lists 40 mm drivers, 47 ohms and a 15 to 20,000 Hz response, and also says the model is currently unavailable from the maker, so check the Amazon listing is new stock before you buy.

How to try them at home

Put the headphones on and play something slow that you know well, such as a hymn or the first piece in your course. Listen for three things. Can you hear the quiet notes at the end of a phrase, or do they vanish? Does the left hand sound like a supporting voice, or does it boom? And after twenty minutes, are your ears and the top of your head still comfortable? A pair that passes those three tests is the right pair, whatever the label says. If your piano has a second jack, ask a friend to put the other pair on and tell you what they hear.

Which should you buy?

If you want the plain choice, the HD 280 Pro. If you want a classic you can find in many studios, the M50x. If your piano's jack seems quiet, the DT 770 Pro X. Whichever you choose, buy from a seller with a return policy, so you can wear them for an evening before you decide.

Questions adults ask

Are closed-back headphones better for piano practice?

For most adults, yes. They keep the sound in and the room out, which suits an apartment or night practice.

Do I need expensive headphones?

No. Comfort and a clear middle matter more than the price.

Do headphones change how I play?

They change what you hear. A pair with a boomy bass can make the left hand sound louder than it is, so try yours with a recording you know.
