What the First Year of Piano Actually Looks Like

Not a promise and not a syllabus — a description, so you can tell whether what is happening to you is normal. Usually it is.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  December 30, 2026

Adults learning alone have no idea what is normal. There is no class to compare with, so every difficulty feels like evidence of a personal limitation, and every quiet stretch feels like failure.

So here is a description of a typical first year at a realistic pace — four or five sessions a week, twenty to thirty minutes, working through a course in order. Your months will not land exactly here. The shape is what to pay attention to.

Months 1–2: everything is new and everything works

You find middle C, learn a five-finger position, and read your first notes on the staff. Both hands play, though usually not together yet. Pieces are short and you finish them, which is why these weeks feel wonderful.

The main risk in this period is going too fast — skipping ahead because the early lessons feel easy, and arriving at month four with gaps in the reading that are hard to go back and fill.

Month 3: the hands-together wall

The first real difficulty, and it arrives for everyone. Each hand knows its part; together they will not go. Adults take this personally more than children do, because it is the first thing in years they cannot do by concentrating harder.

Concentrating harder is precisely what does not work. It is hands separately, then a few bars at a time, very slowly, for a couple of weeks. Then one day it simply goes, and afterwards you cannot reconstruct why it was difficult.

Months 4–5: reading catches up, or does not

The fork in the road. By now you have played enough pieces that you can partly remember them, and the temptation is to learn by ear and by finger memory and let the page become decoration.

It is faster in the short run and it costs a year later. The people who are reading comfortably at month twelve are the ones who, in months four and five, kept reading things they had never heard — a line from a piece they were not studying, a page from the back of the book — even though it was slower and less satisfying.

Months 6–7: the plateau that is not one

This is where most adults stop, and it is the cruelest part of the year, because nothing is actually wrong. The pieces stop getting noticeably harder, your ear has improved enough to hear your own faults clearly, and the gap between what you want and what you produce is at its widest.

It feels like standing still. Underneath, tone and reading and recovery are all still moving — they are simply invisible. This is the stretch to get through on habit rather than enthusiasm, and it is why the ten-minute minimum matters more here than anywhere.

Months 8–9: chords and the left hand wake up

Chords, chord changes, sometimes the first use of the pedal. The left hand stops being a passenger and starts doing something interesting, and pieces begin to sound full rather than thin. Many people say this is when it started sounding like real music.

It is also when the gains from month six become visible, which is no coincidence.

Months 10–12: you have repertoire

Two or three pieces you can play without the music, at any time, reasonably well. Enough reading to sit down with something new at your level and get through it slowly. Enough control to make a melody sing over an accompaniment.

You are not playing Chopin. You are a person who plays the piano, which is a different and much larger change than it sounds.

What is reasonable to expect after twelve months

Simple pieces played well, hands together, from the page. Basic sight-reading in the middle range. Common chords and changes between them. An ear that notices unevenness. And — the part nobody mentions in the advertisements — the ability to keep going after a mistake, which is what makes it possible to play in front of another human being.

If your year has looked roughly like this, including the bleak stretch in the middle, it has been an entirely normal year. The description is not there to grade you. It is there so that in month six you know what month six is.
