How to Start Sight-Reading: A Method You Can Begin This Week

You do not get better at reading by learning pieces. You get better by reading things you will never learn.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  January 8, 2027

Every lesson in the free course reads from the page, in order, from the first note: Start the free course →

Here is something that surprises adults: the person who can play a difficult piece beautifully and the person who can sit down and read a new one are not necessarily the same person. They are two skills, trained two ways, and almost every self-taught adult trains only the first.

Learning a piece means reading a few bars and then repeating them until your hands know them. After a week you are not reading at all — you are remembering. That is fine, it is how pieces get learned, but it builds reading about as much as memorizing a paragraph builds literacy.

The rule that makes it work

Read things you will never learn. Five minutes a day, a piece you have not seen, played once, then never opened again.

That “never again” is the entire method. The moment you repeat it, you have stopped reading and started learning, and the exercise has quietly turned into something else.

Choose music far below your level

This is where adults sabotage themselves. Sight-reading material should be much easier than what you play — roughly two levels down. If you are working through the middle of a beginner course, read from its first weeks.

The aim is fluency, not challenge. You want the experience of the eye moving forward steadily while the hands keep up, because that is the thing being trained. A piece that stops you every two bars trains stopping.

The four rules of the five minutes

Look before you play. Ten seconds. What key, what time signature, what is the lowest and highest note, are there accidentals, does anything repeat? You are not reading the notes yet — you are getting the shape of the page.

Choose a tempo you can hold at the hardest bar. Find the busiest measure, imagine playing it, and set the speed from that. Nearly everyone starts too fast, because the first bar is easy.

Do not stop and do not go back. This is the rule that matters most. Wrong note — keep going. Missed bar — keep going. Stopping to correct is precisely the habit you are trying to break, and in real playing, going back is worse than a wrong note.

Do not look at your hands. You cannot read and watch at the same time. Feel for the keys. You will be surprised how much the hand already knows.

What improves, and when

For about two weeks it feels like nothing is happening. Then you notice you are seeing groups instead of single notes — a chord as a shape, a run as a direction. Then intervals stop being counted and start being recognized, which is the point at which reading speeds up sharply.

Five minutes a day. Not thirty minutes on Sunday: the skill is built by frequency, like vocabulary, and it fades quickly when you stop.

Where to find material

Anything below your level — the early pages of any beginner method, hymn books, easy folk-song collections, children’s albums by real composers. The public-domain libraries hold more free easy music than you could read in a decade.

One page a day, once each, never twice. That is the whole method, and in three months you will read things that stop you today.
