You Can Play All the Right Notes and It Still Sounds Wrong

You have the notes. You have the rhythm. And it still does not sound like music. This is the most common thing adults describe to me, and it has a specific cause.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  October 16, 2026

The free course teaches the sound alongside the notes, from Lesson 1: Open the free course →

An adult wrote to me last month with a sentence I have now heard hundreds of times, in various forms: “I am playing every note correctly and it sounds nothing like the recording.”

They were not imagining it, and it was not a talent problem. It is the predictable result of how adults are taught to play the piano, and it is worth being exact about, because once you can name it you can work on it.

What method books actually teach

Open any of the best-selling adult piano methods and follow what each page asks of you. Page after page, the instruction is: this symbol means this key; put this finger on it; hold it this long.

That is a complete description of which keys go down and when. It is not a description of what the sound should be, and the two are not the same thing. A pianola plays every note correctly, in time, and nobody would sit through a recital of one.

So an adult works honestly through such a book, does everything asked, and arrives at a state where the notes are right, the counting is right, and the result is flat. Nothing in the book was wrong. Something was simply absent from it.

What is missing, mechanically

Here is the thing nobody tells beginners, and it explains most of the rest.

Inside the piano, the hammer is thrown at the string and then flies free. It is not connected to your finger at the moment it strikes. Once it is in flight, nothing you do can change that note — not pressing harder, not holding on, not willing it. The only thing your body controls is how fast the key went down, because that is what decides how fast the hammer travels.

Follow that through and several beliefs collapse at once:

  • You cannot press a note louder. You can only move the key faster. Squeezing produces tension, not volume.
  • Speed of the key comes from the arm, not from the finger working alone. The finger directs weight; the arm supplies it.
  • The only musical decisions available to you are how fast each key descends, and exactly when each one goes down and comes up. That is the entire instrument. Everything expressive is made of those two things.

A book that teaches which key and how long has given you one of the two. The other one — how fast, and therefore how loud and how colored — is the part that makes it music, and it is normally left to a teacher standing beside you.

The four things that change it

1. Listen to a single note all the way out. Play one note with the weight of the arm, and do not play anything else until the sound has completely gone. Most adults have never once listened to the end of a note; they are already thinking about the next one. Everything about tone starts here, and it takes ninety seconds.

2. Make the phrase go somewhere. Music moves toward something and then settles. Take four bars and decide out loud which note is the arrival point, then play it so that everything leans toward that note and relaxes after it. This one change does more for how you sound than a month of new notes.

3. Let one hand be quieter than the other. If the melody and the accompaniment come out at the same volume, the result is mud — and it is exactly what a correct, evenly played beginner performance produces. Play the left hand deliberately softer. It will feel exaggerated. It will not sound exaggerated.

4. Stop the notes deliberately. When a note ends is a musical decision, not an accident of when your finger happens to leave the key. Adults think about starting notes and almost never about ending them, and it is half of what separates playing that sounds finished from playing that sounds approximate.

Why this is not an advanced subject

It is usually treated as one — something to worry about after the notes are secure. I think that is the wrong way round, and it is the single biggest structural fault in adult piano teaching.

The way you produce a sound becomes a habit within weeks. An adult who spends a year pressing keys and then tries to add tone is not adding something; they are undoing something. Everyone I have ever retaught went through that, and it is slow and discouraging work that was entirely avoidable.

Teaching the sound from the first note is not a refinement. It is the cheaper order to learn in.

What to do this week

Take a piece you can already play correctly — anything, however simple. Do not learn anything new. Instead:

  • Play the first note alone and listen until it has gone.
  • Decide which note in the first phrase is the arrival, and play toward it.
  • Play it once with the left hand deliberately quiet.
  • Record it, and then record the version you played last week, and listen to both.

It is the same notes. That is the point — you are not missing information, you are missing a dimension nobody handed you.
