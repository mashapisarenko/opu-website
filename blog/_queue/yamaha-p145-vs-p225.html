Yamaha P-145 vs P-225: Which Should You Buy?

Same keys, different engines. Here is the spec-by-spec comparison and my verdict for adult beginners.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 16, 2026

Forty-one lessons in order, free, at your own pace: Start the free course →

The Yamaha P-225 has more polyphony (192 vs 64), more voices (24 vs 10), two headphone jacks and a slightly richer speaker setup. The P-145BT has the same 88-key GHC keyboard. For most adult beginners, the P-145BT is enough; choose the P-225 if you want more headroom.

My pick for most adults: Yamaha P-145BT

Same 88-key GHC keyboard as the P-225, in a body Yamaha lists at 11.1 kg. If your goal is to learn to play, the key action is the part you cannot upgrade later, and both pianos have the same one.

What is the difference between the Yamaha P-145 and P-225?

The keyboard is the same type on both: Yamaha lists an 88-key GHC keyboard with matte black keytops and the same hard, medium, soft and fixed touch response settings. The differences are in the sound engine and the connections. That is good news for a beginner, because the part your hands touch is the same.

Is more polyphony worth it?

Polyphony is the number of notes that can sound at once. Pedaled playing uses it quickly, because sustained notes keep counting after your fingers leave the keys. At 64 notes, the P-145BT is plenty for the early lessons, simple pieces and most beginner repertoire. At 192, the P-225 has a lot more headroom for layered, pedal-heavy pieces. If you plan to progress to Chopin and Debussy, the P-225 is the safer choice, but the P-145BT will not hold you back for a long time.

Do I need two headphone jacks?

Two jacks let a teacher, partner or friend listen with you, which is useful for lessons at home and duet playing. If you live alone, one is plenty. For headphones, see the best headphones for digital piano; the Sennheiser HD 280 Pro is my starting pair.

How do the two sound and feel in daily practice?

With the same keyboard type, the daily experience at the keys is similar. The difference shows when you use the pedal on fuller pieces, when you want to layer a second sound, or when you connect the piano to a computer for recording: the P-225 lists a 24-bit USB audio interface, the P-145BT a 16-bit one. For a beginner sending recordings to a teacher, either is more than enough.

Which should I buy: the P-145BT or the P-225?

Yamaha P-145BT

Who it is for: The adult beginner who wants the right keys without paying for extras.

Same keyboard type as the P-225, fewer voices, lower polyphony, one headphone jack. Buy this and spend the difference on a proper pedal and an adjustable bench.

Check it on Amazon →

Yamaha P-225

Who it is for: The student who is sure they will stay and likes the idea of more sound and two headphone jacks.

More polyphony, more voices, a 24-bit USB audio interface and a fuller speaker layout. It is the one to buy if you will grow into pedal-heavy repertoire or teach someone alongside you.

Check today's price on Amazon for both; the gap between them changes. If the P-225 is only a small step above the P-145BT on the day, I would take the P-225. If it is a large step, the P-145BT and a good bench is a better use of the money.

How can I decide in five minutes?

Ask three questions. Will you use the pedal heavily on fuller pieces, or play with a teacher or partner beside you? If yes to either, the P-225 earns its place. Do you want the simplest piano that has the key action you need, and plan to spend the difference on a pedal, a bench and headphones? Then the P-145BT is the better use of the money. Is the room small or the piano going to move? Both are about the same size, so weight and width will not decide it. Whichever you choose, you are getting the same keyboard type, and that is the part that teaches your hands.

What else is in the family?

The older Yamaha P-45 uses a graded hammer standard (GHS) keyboard with 64-note polyphony and ten voices. The Roland FP-30X is the main rival; see Roland FP-30X vs Yamaha P-225. For all of them, the digital pianos page is the index, and my general ranking is in the best digital piano for adult beginners.

What accessories should I add?

The footswitch in the box works but does not feel like a pedal. Read the pedal guide, and check which pedals Yamaha lists for your model. A bench at the right height is next: how to choose.

Questions adults ask

Is the Yamaha P-225 better than the P-145?

It has more polyphony (192 vs 64), more voices (24 vs 10) and two headphone jacks. The keyboard type is the same, so for learning, the P-145BT is enough.

Do the P-145 and P-225 have the same keys?

Yamaha lists the same 88-key GHC keyboard with matte black keytops and the same touch response settings on both.

Does the Yamaha P-145BT have Bluetooth?

Yamaha lists Bluetooth audio on the P-145BT, noting that availability varies by area. Check the version you buy.

Does the P-225 come with a sustain pedal?

Yamaha's page lists a footswitch among the included accessories. For better feel, consider a piano-style pedal.

Which Yamaha is better for an adult beginner?

The P-145BT for most adults; the P-225 if you want more headroom and a second headphone jack.
