What to Practice on a Day You Do Not Feel Like Practicing

Not a motivation lecture. One specific, small thing to do on the days when sitting down at the piano feels like too much.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  January 1, 2027

Forty-one lessons in order, free, so you never have to decide what to practice: Start the free course →

Everyone who plays an instrument has these days. You know you should sit down. You do not want to. And the longer you stand in the doorway thinking about it, the larger the whole thing becomes.

What decides whether you are still playing in a year is not what you do on the good days. It is what you do on these ones.

Do not open the piece

On a low day, the worst thing you can do is open the piece you are working on. It is the hardest thing in your repertoire, it will go badly because you are tired, and you will finish the session holding fresh evidence that you are getting worse.

Leave it closed. The piece is not going anywhere.

The ten-minute version

Here is the whole instruction: sit down and play something you already know, slowly, for ten minutes.

Not to improve it. Not to fix anything. Play a piece you finished two months ago, at a comfortable speed, and listen to the sound. That is the entire session, and it is a complete one.

Three things happen. Your hands keep their habits, which is most of what practice is. You end on something that sounded good, so your association with the instrument stays warm. And you did not break the chain, which matters more than any single session’s content.

Why ten minutes is not a consolation prize

Adults treat a short session as a failed long one. It is not. The hand consolidates what it has learned between sessions rather than during them, which is why six ten-minute days beat one hour-long Sunday by a distance.

A ten-minute day is a real day. It is not half a session. It is a whole small one.

Three things worth doing when you have nothing in you

Play one scale, very slowly, and listen to the evenness. It asks almost nothing of your concentration and it works on the thing that is hardest to improve deliberately, which is tone.

Read something far below your level. A piece from the first weeks of the course. Reading improves with volume, and easy reading is the most pleasant volume there is.

Play the piece you started for. Everybody has one — the reason they sat down at a piano in the first place. Play it badly. It does not have to be a practice session to be worth the ten minutes.

The one thing not to do

Do not skip it and promise yourself a long session tomorrow. That trade is never honored, and it turns one missed day into a missed week, which is how most adults stop for good.

If you sit down for ten minutes on the bad days, you will still be playing next year. That is not motivation talking. It is the only reliable difference between the people who keep going and the people who do not.
