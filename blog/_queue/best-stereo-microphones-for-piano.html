Best Pair of Microphones for Piano (Stereo, 2026): A Pianist's Picks

A pair of small-diaphragm condenser microphones is the classic way to record a piano. Here are the pairs I would look at.
By Dr. Maria Pisarenko, DMA  ·  November 28, 2026

The best pair of microphones for recording piano depends on your budget: the RODE M5 matched pair is the lowest-cost pair I would look at, the LEWITT LCT 040 MATCH pair is praised for acoustic instruments, and the RODE NT5 pair is a step up. You also need an audio interface with two microphone preamps and phantom power, such as the Scarlett 2i2.

Quick picks

  • Best value pair: RODE M5 matched pair — a matched pair of small-diaphragm cardioid condensers
  • Best for acoustic instruments: LEWITT LCT 040 MATCH pair — excellent transient response, per the listing
  • Step up: RODE NT5 matched pair — a matched pair with 1/2-inch cardioid capsules

How I choose

A pair has to be matched, so both microphones sound the same. I choose small-diaphragm condensers because they follow the attack of a note. I have not played every instrument or used every device in this guide, and I do not say that I have. What I give you is what I look for as a concert pianist who teaches adults, checked against what each Amazon listing and manufacturer page actually states.

What else you need

An interface with two preamps and phantom power (see Solo vs 2i2), two XLR cables, two stands and a bar or stereo mount. Place the pair facing the open lid, about an arm's length away, and move them until it sounds like the piano in the room. See my recording guide.

Questions adults ask

What microphones do I need for piano?

A matched pair of small-diaphragm condensers, an interface with two preamps and phantom power, and two stands.

Do I need a matched pair?

For stereo recording, yes. Matched means both microphones respond the same way.

Where do I put the microphones?

Facing the open lid, about an arm's length away; move them until it sounds right.
