Casio Privia PX-S1100 Review: A Piano Teacher's Assessment (2026)

A note on method. This is an assessment, not a hands-on test. I have not used this particular piano for weeks. I read Casio's specification page and the Amazon listing as a piano teacher, and tell you what I look for and whether I would recommend it.

My verdict: I would recommend the PX-S1100 to an adult who has a small room, shares a space, or wants to run a piano on batteries. The slim body is the reason to buy it; check the stability of the keyboard under firm chords if you can.

In short

  • Verdict: Casio Privia PX-S1100 — a good choice for small spaces, with two headphone jacks and a damper pedal in the box

What Casio says

Casio lists 88 keys with Smart Scaled Hammer Action, five touch sensitivity levels plus off, 192-note polyphony, 18 tones from its AiR sound source, 8 W plus 8 W amplifiers, two headphone jacks, USB type A and B, Bluetooth 5.0 audio (and MIDI via the included wireless adaptor), an SP-3 damper pedal in the box, and a weight of 11.2 kg without batteries. It measures 1,322 x 232 x 102 mm without the music stand, and runs on six AA batteries or an adaptor.

What I look for, and how it answers

  • Scaled action. Heavier bass keys, lighter treble keys, like a piano. Casio's Smart Scaled Hammer Action is its answer.
  • Polyphony. 192 is plenty for a pedal-heavy nocturne.
  • Two headphone jacks. Good for lessons side by side.
  • A real pedal in the box. An SP-3 is better than a switch.
  • Stability. A very slim body can feel less solid under forceful playing. A good stand helps.

Casio Privia PX-S1100

Who it is for: A small flat, a shared room, or anyone who wants a slim piano that can run on batteries.

What the listing says: Casio lists 88 keys with Smart Scaled Hammer Action, five touch sensitivity levels plus off, 192-note polyphony, 18 tones, 8 W plus 8 W amplifiers, two headphone jacks, Bluetooth 5.0 audio, an SP-3 damper pedal in the box, 11.2 kg, and power by six AA batteries or an adaptor.

What I look for: Slimness can cost key length and stability, so I look at whether the keyboard feels steady under a firm chord and whether the pedal grips. The scaled hammer action puts heavier keys in the bass.

I would suggest it to a student who has told me that space is the problem they need to solve.

Pros
  • Slim and light, with battery power
  • Two headphone jacks and a damper pedal included
Cons
  • Only one pedal jack, so no three-pedal unit on the stock stand
  • A slim body can feel less solid under forceful playing

Buy it if space is the problem you are solving. Skip it if you want the most solid-feeling cabinet or a three-pedal setup.

Questions adults ask

Is the Casio PX-S1100 good for beginners?

In my assessment, yes, especially for small spaces: scaled hammer action, two headphone jacks and a pedal in the box.

Does the PX-S1100 run on batteries?

Casio lists six AA batteries as an option, with about four hours of continuous play, or an AC adaptor.
