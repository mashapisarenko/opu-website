Be honest about the obstacles

What is actually in your way in your thirties

Fifteen minutes is all you have

Fifteen to twenty focused minutes, six days a week, beats one long session. The trick is knowing exactly what to do in them: five minutes slowly on one thing, ten on the smallest broken spot, five playing something you already enjoy.

Apps keep you busy and do not make you better

An app can tell you that you hit the right note. It cannot tell you your hand is tight, your rhythm is rushed or your sound is thin. That is what feedback on a real recording is for.

You quit last time and you do not trust yourself

Almost every adult who quits does so in the first three months, usually because the pieces were too hard or the plan was unclear. We start with a melody on day one so you win early.

You are practicing in a shared home

Digital piano plus headphones solves the noise. The free course is built so that you can practice quietly and still develop real touch.

Guilt about spending time on yourself

Twenty minutes is small enough to protect. Put it at the same time every day and it stops being a decision.

Questions adults ask in their 30s

How long does it take to learn piano as an adult in your 30s?

With twenty minutes of focused practice a day, most adults are playing simple pieces with both hands within a few months. Pieces like Someone Like You or All of Me come when chords, rhythm and reading are each reasonably steady, which the course builds in order.

Is it better to take lessons or learn online?

Both work. A structured free course plus occasional written feedback covers most of what a beginner needs. If you want a live teacher on Zoom, I also teach private lessons.

Can I learn piano if I can't read music?

Yes. You start by ear and by shape, and reading comes in step by step once your hand knows what to do.
