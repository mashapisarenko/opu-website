Be honest about the obstacles

What is actually in your way in your forties

The old teacher is still in the room

Many adults flinch at a wrong note because someone once flinched for them. The cure is a practice style where mistakes are information, and a teacher whose feedback is specific and calm.

Perfectionism

You want the piece to be good immediately, and you notice every flaw. The method here slows you down on purpose, so you build the piece in small parts that are right.

You understand the music faster than your hands can play it

That gap is normal for adults and it is not a sign something is wrong. It closes with the right exercises, not with more hours.

Your hands are tight after a day at a desk

Tension is the most common reason a hand aches at the keyboard. The lessons show where the weight comes from, and the blog covers it in detail. If anything hurts sharply, see a doctor; I am a pianist, not a physician.

No goal, no recital, no exam

Adult learners often drift because nothing is due. Pick one piece from the list as your goal, set a date, and record it.

Questions adults ask in their 40s

Is 45 too old to start piano?

No. Adults learn differently from children, and some things are harder and some are easier. What decides the outcome is whether you are taught the sound and the hand from the first lesson.

I played as a child. Will it come back?

Often faster than you expect. The notes are not gone, but habits are, so we rebuild posture and reading first, then your old pieces.

Can I learn Canon in D or Für Elise as an adult beginner?

Yes, after you can read the page and keep a steady rhythm in both hands. The free course builds those skills in order, and both pieces are common first goals.

Can I learn piano at 40 with no experience?

Yes. Starting at 40 means you bring patience, taste and a clear reason, which children do not have. The free course starts from the first note, so no experience is needed.
