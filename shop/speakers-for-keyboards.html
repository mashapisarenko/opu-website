The speakers built into a small keyboard are rarely the best part of it. A pair of powered monitors can give a digital piano more body and let you hear detail in your own touch. I describe each model by its listing and leave the specifications to the product page.

Why trust a concert pianist's picks?

I recommend this gear because it is what my adult students need to work through my free 41-lesson course and, later, to use with my upcoming method books. I am a concert pianist and a university piano professor, and these are the kinds of tools I look at first when a student asks what to buy.

What should I look for in speakers for a digital piano?

  • Inputs that match your piano, such as RCA or line-level connections.
  • Powered speakers, so no separate amplifier is needed.
  • A sensible size for your desk or room.
  • A faithful sound. You want to hear your touch, not a colored version of it.
  • Stereo. Check whether a listing is for one speaker or a pair.

How do better speakers help you learn?

How this helps you learn: listening to your own tone. Good speakers let you hear touch and evenness, which is what I ask you to listen for in every lesson.

What do players ask about keyboard speakers?

Can I connect a digital piano to studio monitors?

Usually yes, through the piano's line output. Check your piano's manual and the speaker's inputs, and bring the right cable.

Do I need speakers if I use headphones?

No. Speakers give a more open sound and are better when others want to listen.

Should I buy one monitor or a pair?

Stereo needs two. Read each listing to see whether it is a single speaker or a matched pair.

Bookshelf speakers or studio monitors?

Both can work. Studio monitors aim for a faithful sound, and powered bookshelf speakers can be simpler to set up.
