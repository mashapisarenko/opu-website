Why I recommend this gear

I recommend this gear because it is what my adult students need to work through my free 41-lesson course and, later, to use with my upcoming method books. I am a concert pianist and a university piano professor, and these are the kinds of tools I look at first when a student asks what to buy.

How I choose

  • Key count. Sixty-one keys will carry a first year; 88 keys are what real repertoire needs.
  • Touch response. Touch-sensitive keys let loud and soft sound different, which is the beginning of musical playing.
  • Full-size or mini keys. Mini keys are fine for sketching ideas and poor for building a hand.
  • What it connects to. A controller makes no sound by itself; it sends notes to an app or software.
  • Where it will live. Slim and portable is only an advantage if you will really carry it.

How this connects to your learning

How this helps you learn: technique and touch. A keyboard that responds to how you play teaches the hand to shade soft and loud, which is the first musical habit I teach.

A teacher's guide to keyboards and controllers

A portable keyboard and a MIDI controller are different tools. A keyboard makes sound; a controller sends notes to software. Neither is a piano, but both can have a place in a student's life, and it helps to know which job you are hiring them for.

Technique and touch

The first thing I listen for in a beginner's playing is whether soft and loud are different. Touch-sensitive keys make that possible, and keys without it make everything sound the same, however hard the finger works. When a listing says touch-sensitive, that is the detail I care about most.

Mini keys, slim keys, full-size keys

Mini keys are handy for travel and sketching, but they cannot teach a hand how far a real keyboard stretches. If your goal is to play the piano, favor full-size keys, even if the instrument has fewer of them.

When a controller makes sense

If you already practice with apps or music software, a controller is a clean and economical way to play into them. For learning repertoire from my method, I still point students toward an instrument with a real piano sound and weighted keys.

Questions adults ask

Is a 61-key keyboard enough for a beginner?

For the first year, comfortably. It runs out when you open real repertoire, because classical writing uses the extremes of the keyboard. My full answer is in the article on how many keys a beginner needs.

What is a MIDI controller?

A keyboard that sends note information to a computer, tablet or sound module. It has no piano sound of its own unless the listing says it includes sounds, so you need an app or software to hear anything.

Can I learn piano on a 25-key controller?

It is fine for apps and music-making, but it is too small to build a pianist's hand or to play real piano music. Treat it as a tool, not as your piano.

Should I buy a keyboard or a digital piano?

If you can afford it and have the space, a digital piano with 88 weighted keys. If not, choose a keyboard with touch-sensitive keys and plan to upgrade.
