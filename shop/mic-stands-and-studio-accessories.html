Where you put the microphone changes the recording more than the price of the microphone. A good stand lets you place it above the piano or in front of you and leave it there. These are the small fittings I find most useful: stands, boom arms, pop filters for speech, pads for desktop speakers, and a firm keyboard stand. I describe each by its listing.

What should I look for in a mic stand?

  • Tripod base or desk clamp. A tripod stands on the floor; a desk arm clamps to a table and frees the desk.
  • Reach and height. Check the maximum height and boom length for the piano you record.
  • Weight capacity. A heavy microphone needs a heavier stand; read the listing.
  • The thread. Most microphones attach with a standard thread; check the adapter that comes with the stand.
  • Stability. A stand that tips or droops is worse than none.

How do good stands help your recordings?

How this helps you learn: a microphone that stays where you put it gives you recordings you can compare from week to week.

What do students ask about mic stands?

Where should I place a microphone to record piano?

On a boom stand a little above and in front of the instrument, pointing at the strings or the area above the keys. Record a short passage and listen back with headphones before you move it.

Do I need a pop filter for piano?

No. A pop filter is for speech and singing. It is useful if you also record your voice for a lesson or a talk.

What are monitor isolation pads?

Foam pads that sit between a desk and a speaker so the desk does not vibrate. They are a small, cheap fix for a boomy desk.

Can I use a keyboard stand for a digital piano?

Yes, if it holds the weight and width of your instrument. Check the listing for the range of keyboards it fits.
