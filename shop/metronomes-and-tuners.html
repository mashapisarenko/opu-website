Rhythm is the part of reading music that adults find hardest to fix alone, because a pulse that drifts feels steady from the inside. A metronome is the honest witness. I use it with students to check counting, not to chase speed, and the models below cover a classic mechanical metronome, simple digital ones and two tuner combinations, each described only by its own listing.

Why trust a concert pianist's picks?

I recommend this gear because it is what my adult students need to work through my free 41-lesson course and, later, to use with my upcoming method books. I am a concert pianist and a university piano professor, and these are the kinds of tools I look at first when a student asks what to buy.

What should I look for in a metronome?

  • A clear, audible click you can hear over the piano at a moderate volume.
  • Easy tempo changes, because you will move the setting up and down every session.
  • Mechanical or digital. A pendulum needs no screen; digital models add memory and extra sounds.
  • A tuner as well, only if someone in the house plays a string or wind instrument.
  • Power. Rechargeable, battery or wind-up: pick the one you will never have an excuse about.

How does a metronome help you learn piano?

How this helps you learn: steady counting. A metronome shows you where the pulse drifts, so the rhythm you read on the page becomes the rhythm you actually play.

What do students ask about metronomes?

Do I need a metronome to learn piano?

Yes. A steady pulse is what makes written rhythm readable, and a metronome tells you honestly whether yours is steady. Use it to check your counting, then turn it off and keep the beat yourself.

Mechanical or digital metronome: which is better for piano?

Neither plays better. A mechanical metronome such as the Wittner Taktell listed here needs no battery and has no screen. Digital models add memory, extra sounds or rhythm training, as their listings describe.

Is a metronome app on my phone enough?

It works, but a phone at the piano brings messages with it. A separate metronome keeps practice time quiet, which is why I suggest one to adult students.

Do I need a tuner for a digital piano?

No. A digital piano stays in tune. A tuner-metronome combination is useful when someone in the house also plays a string or wind instrument.

What tempo should I start at?

Slow enough to play the passage without a single stop. Raise the setting a little only when it feels easy three times in a row.
