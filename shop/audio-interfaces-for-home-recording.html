An audio interface is the small box between your piano or microphones and your computer. It turns the sound into digital audio, powers condenser microphones, and gives you a headphone output with no delay. You do not need one to begin: a phone is enough for a first recording, and my feedback on your playing is free either way. Buy an interface when you want a recording that sounds like the piano in the room. I describe each interface by what its listing states, and I choose by how simple it is to set up and live with.

What should I look for in an audio interface?

  • Inputs. Two inputs cover a stereo pair of microphones or both outputs of a digital piano; one input is enough for a single microphone.
  • Microphone preamps and phantom power. Condenser microphones need 48-volt phantom power, which the interface supplies.
  • The connection. Check whether the interface uses USB-C or USB-A, and whether the cable you own fits your computer.
  • A headphone output you can monitor on. You should hear yourself without a delay, through headphones, while you record.
  • Software and support. Some interfaces come with recording software; check that your computer's system is supported before you buy.

How does recording help you learn?

How this helps you learn: listening to a clean recording of yourself is the fastest way to hear uneven touch and rhythm, and a clear clip is all I need to give you feedback.

How do I connect a digital piano to an audio interface?

Look at the back of the piano. If it has two output jacks marked left and right, connect each to an input on the interface with a 1/4-inch cable, and set the interface's input to line level. If it has only a headphone jack, a 3.5 mm to 1/4-inch cable works for a clip, though the headphone output is not made for recording. If it has a USB port, you may not need an interface at all.

Record a short test first

Play eight bars, listen back through headphones and check that you hear no hiss and no clipping. Then move on to a whole piece.

What do pianists ask about audio interfaces?

Do I need an audio interface to record my digital piano?

Not always. Many digital pianos have a USB port that sends audio or MIDI to a computer directly. An interface helps when you want to use microphones, record the line outputs of a piano with cables, or listen without delay.

How many inputs do I need to record piano?

Two inputs are enough for most pianists: one for each microphone of a stereo pair, or one for each output of a stereo digital piano. A single input works for one microphone or a mono signal.

What is phantom power?

A 48-volt supply that the interface sends down the microphone cable to power a condenser microphone. Dynamic microphones do not need it.

Which interface is the best for a beginner?

The one you will really set up and use. For a first interface I choose a two-input model from a maker with a long record of software support, and I do not pay for channels I will not use.
