A MIDI keyboard controller is a keyboard that sends notes to software: a virtual piano, a notation program, a recording program or a learning app. It is a tool, not a replacement for a piano with weighted keys. I recommend one to an adult who already records or composes, or who wants a software piano on a desk. If you are choosing your first piano, start at my digital piano pages instead. I describe each controller by what its listing states.

What should I look for in a MIDI keyboard controller?

  • The keys. Weighted hammer-action keys feel like a piano; synth-action keys are lighter and suit software and composing.
  • Key count. 25 and 37 keys fit a travel bag, 49 and 61 keys suit software, and 88 keys suit a pianist.
  • No built-in sound. A controller needs a computer, a tablet or a sound module; check what your listing says it works with.
  • The software it comes with. Some controllers include a virtual piano or recording software.
  • Pads, knobs and sliders. Useful for producers and often not needed by a learner.

How can a MIDI controller help you learn?

How this helps you learn: a controller with piano touch lets you practice into notation or a software piano, and seeing your notes on a screen can show you where your rhythm drifts.

What do learners ask about MIDI controllers?

What is a MIDI keyboard controller?

A keyboard that sends note and control messages to a computer or tablet but does not make sound itself. The software turns those notes into piano or any other sound.

Can I learn piano on a MIDI controller?

Only if it has weighted, piano-style keys and you are also practicing technique. A small synth-action controller is a tool for software, not a piano.

Do I need to buy software with a MIDI controller?

Many controllers include some software; check the listing. Computers and tablets also have free piano sounds and music programs.

How many keys should a MIDI controller have?

49 keys cover most software uses; 88 keys with weighted action suit a pianist who wants the full range.
