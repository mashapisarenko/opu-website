A teacher's guide to recording yourself

Recording yourself is the quickest way to hear what a listener hears. It is also how you can send your playing to me for feedback: I read every message and listen to every recording myself, and the feedback is free.

Start with sound

A handheld recorder or a USB microphone is enough. Place it a little above and in front of the instrument, record one short passage, and listen back. If you can hear the notes clearly, your setup is good enough to send.

Then the picture

If you want to show your hands, a phone on an overhead mount or clamp arm looks down at the keys. A ring light or LED panel gives even light. A wireless clip-on microphone is useful if you also want to speak to the camera, as a teacher does.

Keep it simple

Do not wait for the perfect setup. A phone propped at the side of the keyboard works for a first clip. Better gear can come once recording is a habit.

Questions adults ask

What do I need to record piano at home?

A recorder or microphone for sound and a phone for video. Start with the simplest setup and improve it once you are recording regularly.

USB microphone or audio interface?

A USB microphone plugs straight into a computer. An interface lets you connect other microphones and gear, and gives you more room to grow.

How do I film my hands on the keys?

Use an overhead mount or a clamp arm that holds your phone above the keyboard, as the mount listings describe.

Can I send a recording to my teacher?

Yes. A clear clip is all I need for feedback, and my feedback service is free: send me a video of your playing.
