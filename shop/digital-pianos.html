Why I recommend this gear

I recommend this gear because it is what my adult students need to work through my free 41-lesson course and, later, to use with my upcoming method books. I am a concert pianist and a university piano professor, and these are the kinds of tools I look at first when a student asks what to buy.

How I choose

  • 88 keys if you are buying once and keeping it, so middle C sits where it does on a real piano.
  • Weighted keys, ideally with a hammer-action feel, so soft and loud playing feel different under the fingers.
  • A real piano sound first. Hundreds of other voices are not what you are paying for.
  • A sustain pedal, either included or supported, because the pedal is part of the instrument.
  • A solid stand and an adjustable bench, so the instrument does not move and your forearms sit level.

How this connects to your learning

How this helps you learn: a weighted keyboard is where technique and touch are built, and it is the instrument I picture an adult student practicing on between lessons of the free 41-lesson course.

A teacher's guide to choosing a digital piano

When an adult asks me which digital piano to buy, I answer with questions about the hands, not the model. Do the keys feel different when you press them gently and when you press them with the weight of the arm? Does the piano sound like a piano? Can the instrument stand firmly at the right height, with a real pedal under your foot? Those four questions decide almost everything.

Key action: weighted, semi-weighted, hammer action

Weighted keys resist the finger the way a piano key does. Hammer action goes one step further and imitates the mechanism of an acoustic piano. Semi-weighted keys are lighter, and several listings say so openly. For a student who will work through my free course and later my upcoming method books, I put weighted keys first, because the touch you practice is the touch you will carry to every piano you meet.

Cabinet or portable

A portable digital piano is light enough to move and needs a stand and a bench. A console or upright-style cabinet comes with its own furniture and sits in a room like a piece of furniture. Decide where it will live before you decide what it will be.

What I would try first

If you can, press the keys in a shop. Play one note softly, one note loudly, and listen for a difference. Then check that a sustain pedal is included or supported, and that you have a place for headphones.

Questions adults ask

How many keys should a digital piano have?

If you are buying once and keeping it, choose 88 keys. Sixty-one weighted keys can carry the first year, but real repertoire uses the whole keyboard. My longer answer is in the article on how many keys a beginner needs.

Do I need weighted keys?

Yes, if you can manage it. A weighted key answers differently when you play softly or with the weight of the arm, and that relationship is what piano playing is. Unweighted keys teach a hand to poke.

Do I need a sustain pedal?

Eventually, yes. Much classical writing assumes a pedal, so choose a model that includes one or accepts one, and buy a proper piano-style pedal if the supplied one is a plain switch.

Is a digital piano good enough to learn on?

For an adult beginner, a good digital piano with weighted keys is a sound choice. If you can, try the touch before you commit, and read my comparison of digital and acoustic pianos for what really matters.
